Guarantees — Highest Risk Item

  • 1. Construction completion guarantee
  • 2. Stabilization and conversion guarantee
  • 3. Operating deficit guarantee
  • 4. Tax credit compliance guarantee
  • 5. Environmental indemnities

Key Details

  • Investor (bank) will likely require JOINT AND SEVERAL liability
  • Side agreement between Trinity/Fontaine to make proportional (75/25)
  • Joint & several means lender can pursue Fontaine for 100% regardless of side agreement
  • Side agreement ONLY governs between the partners — useless if Trinity can’t pay
🚨 CRITICAL WARNING

Joint and several on a $119M project means the bank can come after Fontaine for the ENTIRE obligation, not just 25%. If Trinity defaults, Fontaine is on the hook for everything. This COULD destroy bonding capacity.

Risk Matrix

⚠ Red Flags

  1. Joint & several personal guarantees (bonding capacity risk)
  2. Minority partner with no veto/control
  3. GC contract not guaranteed (could lose bid)
  4. Long capital lockup (3–5 years to stabilize)
  5. Tax credit recapture risk
  6. $38.2M funding gap

✓ Green Flags

  1. Union development access (currently shut out)
  2. Move up value chain (builder → development partner)
  3. Political capital (Secretary + Lt. Governor relationship)
  4. Replicable model for future deals
  5. Trinity is experienced (Curtis Apartments track record)
  6. Phase 2 upside (144 more units)
10

Negotiation Recommendations

  1. Hard dollar caps on guarantee exposure (not just percentage)
  2. Lock GC contract or meaningful break fee before committing capital
  3. Consider 10–15% stake with capped guarantees as alternative
  4. Structure through Fontaine-controlled LLC (ring-fence liability from personal assets and surety)
  5. Guarantee release triggers tied to project milestones
  6. Pre-construction capital protected by first-money-out provisions
