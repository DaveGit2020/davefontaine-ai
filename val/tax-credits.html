Dollar-for-dollar off the tax bill. $1 credit = $1 saved.

Bottom line: Credits are ~5x more powerful than deductions at a 21% corporate tax rate. A $50M credit saves $50M. A $50M deduction saves only $10.5M. That is why banks write real checks for credits.

Where the Money Comes From — 7-Step Flow

1

US Government Creates Credits

IRS Allocation

IRS allocates LIHTC credits to every state based on population. Massachusetts gets its share each year. This costs the government $0 upfront — they simply collect less tax revenue later. No appropriation, no budget line item.

2

MassHousing Awards Credits to Trinity

~$49.5M in Credits Awarded

Trinity applies to MassHousing (the state housing finance agency). MassHousing evaluates the project — site, need, financials, developer track record — and awards the credits. Trinity now has the right to generate ~$49.5M in federal tax credits by building 110 affordable apartments.

3

Trinity Hires Syndicator to Shop Credits

Boston Capital / National Syndicator

Trinity hires a syndicator (like Boston Capital, National Equity Fund, Raymond James, etc.) to package the credits and shop them to banks and corporate investors. "We have $49.5M in tax credits. Who wants them?" Banks compete. Going rate: $0.90-$0.95 per credit dollar.

4

Bank (BOA) Pays $50.3M Real Cash

$50,300,000 Cash Invested

Bank pays $50.3M in real cash into the project limited partnership. In exchange, the bank gets 99.99% of tax credits + depreciation losses. The bank has almost zero economic interest in the actual building. They are buying tax benefits, not real estate.

5

Money Builds 110 Apartments

$119M Total Development

Bank's $50.3M + permanent mortgage ($12.5M) + EOHLC subsidy ($8.25M) + MassHousing soft loans ($3.5M) + historic credits ($4.2M) + other sources = enough to build 110 affordable apartments in downtown Worcester. The credits literally fund the construction.

6

Bank Gets Tax Benefits Over 10-15 Years

~$55M Total Return

Over the compliance period, the bank reduces its federal tax bill by ~$49.5M (LIHTC credits), saves ~$3.5M from depreciation deductions (21% of paper losses), and earns ~$2M in CRA compliance value. Total return: ~$55M on $50.3M invested. The bank profits.

7

Year 15+: Bank Exits for $100

Trinity 75% + Fontaine 25% Own Building

After the 15-year compliance period, the bank has used all its credits. They exit the partnership for a nominal $100. Trinity (75%) and Fontaine (25%) now own the building. This is where Dave's real equity lives — a stabilized, cash-flowing, 110-unit asset in Worcester.

The 9% Explained — How Credits Are Calculated

The 9% Credit Rate

The "9%" is a monthly credit rate set by the IRS. It is not a discount or a fee. It determines how many credits the project generates.

Monthly credit rate (IRS-set) ~9%
Credit period 10 years (120 months)
9% × 10 years ~90% of qualified basis
The Math: From Hard Costs to Credits
Total hard costs
$82M
Strip non-eligible
~$65-70M eligible
Affordable fraction
~85% affordable = $55-60M qualified basis
Apply 9% × 10 yrs
~$49.5-54M in total credits

Critical Distinction — Two Different "90%" Numbers

90% from the 9% rate

This determines how many credits exist. 9% monthly rate × 10 years = project generates credits equal to ~90% of its qualified basis.

$0.90-$0.95 per credit dollar

This is what the bank pays per credit dollar. It is a time-value-of-money discount because the bank has to wait 10 years to use all credits.

These are two completely different things. The first creates the credits. The second prices them.

What Drives Credit Pricing ($0.85–$0.98)

Cheaper Credits ($0.85–$0.90)
  • New or unproven developer
  • Risky or declining market
  • Complex project (rehab, environmental)
  • Higher compliance risk
  • Supply glut (too many credits on market)
  • Rising interest rates (higher opportunity cost)
  • Rural or low-demand location
Premium Credits ($0.95–$0.98)
  • Experienced developer (Trinity = premium)
  • Strong market (Worcester trending up)
  • Government subsidy stacking (less risk)
  • High CRA need in geography
  • Low interest rate environment
  • Deal scarcity (more buyers than credits)
  • Strong anchor tenants / lease-up history
340 Main likely prices at ~$0.92–$0.95. Trinity is a top-tier developer, Worcester is a rising market, and the deal stacks multiple government subsidies. The only discount factors are project complexity (adaptive reuse) and current rate environment.

CRA Compliance — Why Banks Must Invest

Community Reinvestment Act (1977)

Federal law requiring banks to serve the communities where they take deposits, especially low- and moderate-income neighborhoods. Banks are examined by regulators every 3-5 years.

CRA Examination Ratings:

Outstanding Satisfactory Needs to Improve Substantial Noncompliance
Consequences of a bad rating: Regulators can BLOCK mergers, deny new branch applications, restrict expansion plans, and create public reputational damage. For a bank like BOA that is constantly acquiring and expanding, a bad CRA rating is an existential threat to their growth strategy.
BOA's CRA Deployment Need
Estimated annual CRA need (national) $5–15 BILLION/year

BOA operates in hundreds of markets. Each market needs CRA investment. A single $50M deal in Worcester is a small piece of their national puzzle, but it checks a critical box in Massachusetts.

LIHTC is the premium CRA investment — the only one that also comes with dollar-for-dollar tax credits. Every other CRA option costs the bank money or returns below market. LIHTC is the only play where the bank satisfies regulators AND makes a profit. That is why there is always demand.
