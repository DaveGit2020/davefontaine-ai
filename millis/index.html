The Backward Planning Approach

GO DEEPER

How it works — we start from Day 0 and work backward:

  • Day 0: Students walk in. Building fully operational.
  • Week -1: Final punchlist, cleaning, furniture
  • Week -2: Commissioning, T&B, envelope testing
  • Week -4: All MEP operational, inspections passed
  • Week -8: Interior fitout, paint & finishes
  • Week -10: Demo & abatement start (summer day 1)

Every trade knows their dates. Every inspection pre-scheduled. Every piece of equipment already on site.

Protecting School Operations

GO DEEPER
  • "Blackout hours" — no deliveries during drop-off/pick-up
  • Segregated circulation — construction never crosses student paths
  • High-vis crosswalks with strobe lights (proven at East Longmeadow)
  • Outdoor recreation maintained every phase
  • IAQ containment — negative air, temp walls. So effective we've been pulled into healthcare.
  • Noise management — quiet hours, loud work scheduled during low-impact windows

Phase 1 — New Addition (Mar 2027 – Jul 2028)

New middle school wing and gymnasium. Steel erection May-Jul 2027. Envelope tight Jan 2028. Substantial completion July 14, 2028.

Phase 2 — Locker Rooms & Boiler (Summer Slammer #1)

10-week window. The boiler room is the heart — new systems must chase back to this renovated core. Hazmat, demo, underslab, roof, full fitout.

Phase 3 — Gym, Admin, Cafe (Summer Slammer #2)

Most operationally sensitive summer. Students transition to new addition. VDC at fabrication level. Shutdown rehearsals. No surprises.

Phase 4 — Classroom Wing (Jun 2028 – Feb 2029)

Largest renovation concentration. 1960s CMU walls, solar panels, roof strategy decisions.

Phase 5 — Demo Wing & HS Entry (Feb – Aug 2029)

Hazmat abatement (30 days), building demo (30 days), new HS entry construction.

Phase 5A — Cafeteria, Theater & Music (Summer Slammer #3)

Final renovations. Project transitions from construction to operations. Completion: August 31, 2029.

Case Study: South High — Underslab Drainage

GO DEEPER

Challenge: Design included underslab drainage not in coordination models. Structural model missing foundations.

Solution: Modeled ALL foundations + drew underslab drainage, rerouting piping around foundations.

Result: Zero conflicts during installation. Dimensioned drawings produced for site contractor.

Case Study: South High — Storm Drain Beam

GO DEEPER

Challenge: 150' roof drains needed beam penetration. Design showed pipe with no pitch.

Solution: Modeled correct pitch, identified penetration before steel fabrication.

Result: Structural engineer adjusted in design. Zero rework.
